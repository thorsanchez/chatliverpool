Liverpool FC 2024/25 Premier League Season

Premier League Match Summaries

August 2024
17 Aug – Ipswich Town 0–2 Liverpool: Arne Slot’s tenure began with a routine away win against newly promoted Ipswich. After a goalless first half, Diogo Jota broke the deadlock and Mohamed Salah sealed the 2–0 victory with a 65th-minute strike

. Slot thus became the first Liverpool manager since 1998 to win his Premier League debut match

.
25 Aug – Liverpool 2–0 Brentford: Liverpool marked their first home game of the season with a comfortable 2–0 win over Brentford. Luis Díaz opened the scoring in the first half and Salah doubled the lead after the break, ensuring a second consecutive clean-sheet victory at Anfield.
September 2024
1 Sep – Manchester United 0–3 Liverpool: In Slot’s first major test, Liverpool earned an emphatic 3–0 win at Old Trafford

. Cody Gakpo and Salah scored in the first half, and substitute Darwin Núñez added a late third. Slot became only the second Liverpool manager ever to win on his first visit to Man United away

, underlining the team’s flying start.
14 Sep – Liverpool 0–1 Nottingham Forest: Liverpool’s perfect start was halted by a shock 0–1 home defeat to Nottingham Forest. A first-half goal by the visitors (capitalizing on a defensive lapse) proved decisive as Liverpool struggled to break down Forest’s deep defense. This upset was the only blemish in an otherwise strong early run

.
21 Sep – Liverpool 3–0 AFC Bournemouth: The Reds bounced back convincingly, sweeping Bournemouth aside 3–0 at Anfield. Dominik Szoboszlai put Liverpool ahead with a long-range effort, before Jota and Salah each netted to secure the three points. Liverpool’s attack was in full flow, with Salah tormenting the Cherries’ back line in a dominant display.
28 Sep – Wolves 1–2 Liverpool: Liverpool earned a hard-fought 2–1 victory away at Wolves. Goals from Luis Díaz and Alexis Mac Allister gave the Reds a two-goal cushion, before Wolves pulled one back late. Despite some nervy moments in the final minutes, Liverpool’s defense held firm to take the win at Molineux.
October 2024
5 Oct – Crystal Palace 0–1 Liverpool: At Selhurst Park, Liverpool ground out a 1–0 win over Crystal Palace. In a tight contest, Salah’s penalty in the second half was the difference. The Reds showed resilience and game management, preserving the clean sheet under pressure from Palace’s aerial attack.
20 Oct – Liverpool 2–1 Chelsea: After an international break, Liverpool edged Chelsea 2–1 at Anfield. Goals from Salah and Szoboszlai put Liverpool 2–0 up by halftime. Chelsea fought back with a goal from Raheem Sterling, but Liverpool’s organized defense and Alisson’s saves ensured Slot’s men took all three points.
27 Oct – Arsenal 2–2 Liverpool: In a high-profile clash at the Emirates, Liverpool drew 2–2 with Arsenal. The Reds twice took the lead – first through Díaz and later via a Salah strike – but Arsenal equalized both times. It was a pulsating encounter between title contenders, and the draw kept Liverpool unbeaten in October. Slot’s flexible tactics were on display as Liverpool balanced attacking ambition with defensive discipline to earn a point in London.
November 2024
2 Nov – Liverpool 2–1 Brighton: Liverpool continued their strong form, beating Brighton 2–1 at home. An early goal from Gakpo and a second-half header by Virgil van Dijk gave Liverpool a two-goal lead, before Brighton pulled one back late. The win kicked off November on a positive note.
9 Nov – Liverpool 2–0 Aston Villa: The Reds secured a comfortable 2–0 victory over Aston Villa at Anfield. Jota and Salah each scored in a dominant first-half performance. Liverpool controlled 56.8% of possession and piled on pressure with numerous final-third entries



. This result marked Liverpool’s 10th league win, and by now Slot’s side had established themselves as the early pace-setters in the title race.
24 Nov – Southampton 2–3 Liverpool: In a thriller at St. Mary’s, Liverpool prevailed 3–2 against Southampton. After falling behind early, Liverpool responded with goals from Mac Allister, Salah, and Díaz. Southampton made it 3–2 in the 80th minute, but Liverpool’s midfield duo of Mac Allister and Ryan Gravenberch broke up attacks effectively to protect the lead

. The hard-fought win demonstrated the team’s character and kept their winning momentum. (Note: This match was originally scheduled earlier but took place in late November.)
By the November international break, Slot’s Liverpool had remarkably won 11 of their first 12 league games

. This blistering start – the best by any new Liverpool manager – earned Slot the Premier League Manager of the Month award for November 2024

. Liverpool’s attacking play was free-flowing and aggressive, while their defense had conceded only six goals in the first 11 matches



.
December 2024
1 Dec – Liverpool 2–0 Manchester City: Liverpool opened December by defeating the reigning champions Man City 2–0 at Anfield. In a statement win, Salah starred with a goal and an assist, exploiting City’s high line on the break

. Trent Alexander-Arnold, returning from a minor knock, helped supply Salah with progressive passes from deep



. This victory extended Liverpool’s lead at the top and showcased Slot’s ability to go toe-to-toe with elite opposition.
4 Dec – Newcastle United 3–3 Liverpool: In a wild midweek encounter, Liverpool drew 3–3 away at Newcastle. The Reds raced to a 2–0 lead (through Salah and Díaz), but defensive lapses saw Newcastle rally to 2–2. Darwin Núñez scored to make it 3–2 for Liverpool, only for Newcastle to equalize in stoppage time. Despite dropping two points, Liverpool’s attack again shone, and Salah’s performance – including two assists – kept him atop the league’s assist charts.
14 Dec – Liverpool 2–2 Fulham: Liverpool were held 2–2 by Fulham at Anfield. A sluggish start saw Fulham go ahead, before goals from Szoboszlai and Salah put Liverpool in front. However, a late Fulham equalizer meant Liverpool had to settle for a draw. The result highlighted some defensive complacency, though it extended Liverpool’s unbeaten run to nine games since September.
22 Dec – Tottenham Hotspur 3–6 Liverpool: In one of the season’s most extraordinary matches, Liverpool overpowered Spurs 6–3 in London. Mohamed Salah recorded a hat-trick – scoring the 5th goal to complete it

 – and Luis Díaz netted a brace as the Reds ran riot. The game was “dominant but chaotic” with end-to-end action

. Salah’s two goals in seven second-half minutes during this match saw him climb to fourth on Liverpool’s all-time scoring list

. By dismantling Spurs, Liverpool sent a message about their attacking prowess.
26 Dec – Liverpool 3–1 Leicester City: On Boxing Day, Liverpool dispatched Leicester 3–1 at Anfield. Goals from Gakpo, Jota, and Díaz secured the win in a match where Liverpool largely cruised after building a 3–0 lead. Leicester’s consolation goal was one of the rare times Liverpool’s defense was breached at home in the first half of the season.
29 Dec – West Ham United 0–5 Liverpool: Liverpool ended 2024 in style with a 5–0 demolition of West Ham in London. The Reds scored five past the Hammers, including a Salah double and strikes from Díaz, Gakpo, and Van Dijk (from a set-piece). It was Liverpool’s biggest win of the season. This result meant Slot’s side entered the new year nine points clear at the top of the Premier League table

, capping an outstanding first half of the campaign.
January 2025
5 Jan – Liverpool 2–2 Manchester United: The new year began with a pulsating 2–2 draw against rivals Man United at Anfield. Liverpool led 2–0 at halftime thanks to goals from Salah and Curtis Jones, but United fought back with two second-half goals. It was a bitter reminder that no lead is safe, though Liverpool’s unbeaten streak continued.
14 Jan – Nottingham Forest 1–1 Liverpool: In the return fixture at the City Ground, Liverpool were held 1–1 by Nottingham Forest. Luis Díaz’s early goal was canceled out by a Forest equalizer. The Reds pushed hard (Salah hit the post late on) but could not find a winner against a stubborn Forest side that proved to be a bogey team.
18 Jan – Brentford 0–2 Liverpool: Liverpool got back to winning ways, beating Brentford 2–0 in London. First-half goals from Salah and Díaz put the game out of reach early. The team’s high press caused Brentford problems all game, with Szoboszlai and Mac Allister energetically winning the midfield battles.
25 Jan – Liverpool 4–1 Ipswich Town: The Reds convincingly defeated Ipswich 4–1 at Anfield, completing the double over the newcomers. Cody Gakpo scored twice, while Jota and Salah each added a goal. Notably, this match set a season-high attendance of 60,420 at Anfield

. Liverpool’s attack was in full flow, carving Ipswich open repeatedly.
February 2025
1 Feb – AFC Bournemouth 0–2 Liverpool: Liverpool continued their march with a professional 2–0 win away at Bournemouth. Goals in each half from Darwin Núñez and Salah secured the points. The team “maintained the width” through wingers and frequently attacked 1v1 down the flanks

, a hallmark of Slot’s approach that paid off against the Cherries.
12 Feb – Everton 2–2 Liverpool: In the Merseyside Derby at Goodison Park, honors were even in a 2–2 draw. Liverpool overcame a 0–1 deficit with goals from Salah and Gakpo to lead 2–1, but Everton grabbed a late equalizer. The frenetic derby saw challenges flying in; Liverpool’s high challenge intensity (5.7 duels per minute of opponent possession) was evident as they pressed aggressively

. Ultimately, a draw kept Liverpool’s unbeaten league run alive, though their lead atop the table narrowed slightly.
16 Feb – Liverpool 2–1 Wolves: Back at Anfield, Liverpool edged Wolverhampton 2–1. Mohamed Salah and Curtis Jones scored in a game where Liverpool had to work hard to break down Wolves. After conceding a goal due to a defensive mix-up, the Reds tightened up, with Gravenberch making key interceptions in midfield

 to prevent a Wolves equalizer.
19 Feb – Aston Villa 2–2 Liverpool: Liverpool were held to a 2–2 draw at Villa Park. Despite goals from Diogo Jota and Szoboszlai, defensive lapses allowed Villa to score twice, including a late leveling goal. Slot’s men showed frustration at dropping points, but remained unbeaten in the league since the turn of the year.
23 Feb – Manchester City 0–2 Liverpool: In a massive result at the Etihad, Liverpool beat Man City 2–0, completing a league double over the champions. An inspired Salah scored one goal and assisted another, becoming the first player ever to score and assist in both league games against the reigning champions in a season

. Liverpool’s midfield pressed in a 4-2-4 shape off the ball



, disrupting City’s build-up and leading to turnovers that the Reds capitalized on. This win reinforced Liverpool’s title credentials and widened the gap to City in the standings.
26 Feb – Liverpool 2–0 Newcastle United: Liverpool closed out February with a 2–0 home win over Newcastle. Goals from Luis Díaz and Trent Alexander-Arnold (a rare strike from a free-kick) secured the victory. The team’s well-drilled mid-block press trapped Newcastle effectively



, and Liverpool earned their third consecutive clean sheet in the league.
March 2025
8 Mar – Liverpool 3–1 Southampton: Liverpool dispatched Southampton 3–1 at Anfield. After conceding an early goal, the hosts rallied with a quick-fire double from Salah (one goal, one assist) to lead at halftime. Diogo Jota’s goal in the second half put the game beyond Southampton. Impressively, one Liverpool goal came within 15 seconds of a defensive recovery – highlighting the team’s lethal transition play reminiscent of Klopp’s era



. This win kept Liverpool firmly on course at the top, with their unbeaten league run stretching into late March.
April 2025
2 Apr – Liverpool 1–1 Everton: The second Merseyside Derby of the season, played at Anfield, ended in a 1–1 draw. Salah’s early goal (extending his scoring streak in Sunday matches) was cancelled out by Everton in the second half. Despite hitting the woodwork twice, Liverpool could not find a winner. It was a physical encounter that saw a cautious approach from Slot’s side in the final minutes to avoid risking defeat.
6 Apr – Fulham 3–2 Liverpool: Liverpool suffered a rare defeat in a 3–2 thriller at Craven Cottage. Fulham’s counter-attacking pace troubled the league leaders as Liverpool found themselves 0–2 down by halftime. Salah and Gakpo scored to bring Liverpool level at 2–2, but a late Fulham goal handed Liverpool their first league loss since September. This marked only their second defeat of the campaign and ended a 23-match unbeaten league run.
13 Apr – Liverpool 2–1 West Ham United: Liverpool bounced back to beat West Ham 2–1 at Anfield. Cody Gakpo and Luis Díaz scored in a dominant first half. Notably, Salah provided the assist for Díaz’s goal – his 18th league assist of the season

 – setting a new club record for assists in a Premier League campaign

. West Ham pulled one goal back, but Liverpool managed the game well in the closing stages to secure the win.
20 Apr – Leicester City 0–1 Liverpool: Liverpool eked out a 1–0 win away at Leicester. A lone goal from substitute Darwin Núñez in the 70th minute proved enough in a match where Liverpool rotated a few players. Caoimhín Kelleher started in goal as Alisson was still recovering (see Injuries section), and he kept a clean sheet with two crucial saves. The victory at the King Power Stadium inched Liverpool closer to mathematically sealing the Premier League title.
(Matches on 27 Apr and beyond fall outside the scope of this summary, as they had not been played at the time of writing.)

Tactical and Managerial Insights under Arne Slot

Arne Slot’s appointment as Liverpool manager in June 2024 ushered in a new tactical era at the club

. Formations and Fluidity: Slot shunned rigid formation labels, instead promoting fluid positional play. Nominally, Liverpool often lined up in a 4-2-3-1 shape with the ball (a shift from Jürgen Klopp’s 4-3-3)



. However, Slot emphasized that when in possession, players have freedom to interchange positions. “Sometimes it is a 4-2-3-1; sometimes it is a 4-1-4-1; sometimes we build up with three,” Slot explained of his philosophy



. This meant that, for example, Trent Alexander-Arnold would occasionally tuck into midfield during build-up (forming a back three with the center-backs) while the wingers pushed high and wide. Attacking Approach: Liverpool played proactive, front-foot football. The team averaged 56.8% possession in the first 11 league games

 – third-highest in the league – but it was purposeful possession aimed at progressing forward. Slot’s side ranked among the top teams for passes into the final third and progressive passes

. The wingers maintained width, with Salah on the right and usually Díaz or Gakpo on the left hugging the touchlines to stretch defenses

. This created space for 1v1 take-ons – Salah (57) and Díaz (45) were both top-30 in the league for dribbles attempted

. With width provided, the center-forward (No.9) – whether Diogo Jota, who excels at finding pockets of space, or Darwin Núñez, who pins defenders with his physicality – occupied the center-backs. This in turn opened lanes for midfield runners from deep. Slot encouraged his advanced midfielders (often Curtis Jones and Alexis Mac Allister) to make vertical runs beyond the striker

. Jones in particular frequently burst into the box, capitalizing on the space created. Meanwhile, Ryan Gravenberch was deployed as the single pivot (No.6), sitting deepest in midfield. Gravenberch would drop between or beside the center-backs in build-up, especially against teams defending in a low block, to help progress the ball forward

. One hallmark of Slot’s attack was flexibility. Players rotated positions in possession – for instance, Dominik Szoboszlai, usually an attacking midfielder, sometimes drifted wide or high like a second striker, while Mac Allister might temporarily hold position to let others roam. Slot said he hoped people would see “a lot of freedom when we have the ball” rather than a fixed formation

. This fluid movement made Liverpool harder to predict and allowed creative players like Salah to roam into goal-scoring pockets. Crucially, though, Slot balanced freedom with structure: “When we don’t have the ball there is not so much freedom. We want to be really aggressive and everybody mostly keeps his position,” he noted

. In other words, off-ball discipline was key. Defensive Organization and Pressing: Out of possession, Liverpool under Slot were aggressive yet calculated. The team often defended in a 4-4-2 or 4-2-4 shape when pressing



. Typically, one of the central midfielders (frequently Szoboszlai) would push up alongside the striker to form a front two in the press

. The wingers, Salah and Díaz, would narrow in from the flanks, effectively creating a front four that pressed the opposing backline and defensive midfield. By doing so, they aimed to cut off wide passing options – the wingers’ inward movement prevented opponents from easily playing out to the flanks

. This tactic specifically limited opponents’ midfielders from receiving the ball in the half-spaces; it forced play into central traps where Liverpool’s midfielders could aggressively contest possession. In the first few months, Liverpool’s pressing stats were impressive – they were joint-third in the league for “challenge intensity,” averaging 5.7 defensive actions (duels, tackles, interceptions) per minute of opponent possession

. They also had the 4th-lowest PPDA (passes allowed per defensive action), reflecting a preference for engaging opponents higher up the pitch

. However, Slot’s press was slightly more measured than Klopp’s famously frenetic gegenpress. Observers noted it was “not the extremely aggressive high-pressing of the Klopp era” but still a very purposeful press

. Liverpool would often press from a mid-block – starting with a compact 4-4-2 shape and then springing the press when the ball entered certain areas or when a trigger (like a poor touch) was spotted

. Slot’s Liverpool thus “jumped with purpose” rather than pressing relentlessly at all times

. The two deeper midfielders (usually Mac Allister and Gravenberch) provided a safety net, staying compact to protect the space in front of the defense



. Both Mac Allister and Gravenberch excelled in these roles – by mid-season Mac Allister ranked 23rd in the league for total defensive duels (82) and Gravenberch 28th (78), and Gravenberch was among the top interceptors (13th with 57 interceptions)

. Their work rate and positional sense helped Liverpool regain possession and snuff out counterattacks. This balance meant Liverpool remained defensively solid (only 6 goals conceded in the first 11 matches) while still deploying an aggressive press

. Transitions: Like Klopp’s team, Slot’s Liverpool showed lethal ability in transition. They often turned high presses or midfield steals into quick strikes. By November, Liverpool had scored 9 open-play goals within 15 seconds of winning the ball back

. This included goals that originated from defending a corner and immediately countering downfield

. Slot clearly maintained the club’s ethos of pouncing on transitions; he allowed players like Salah and Díaz to remain in advanced positions during defensive phases so that they could break forward instantly upon a turnover

. Salah in particular was given license to stay high and wide when Liverpool were defending, to be a ready outlet. As a result, Salah has been involved in countless fast breaks – as noted, he has arguably been the Premier League’s most devastating winger on the transition, a fact Slot fully capitalized on

. One vivid example was the away win at Manchester United, where Liverpool stayed compact around the half-way line, then burst forward once they won the ball – Salah and Díaz were “outstanding in 1v1 moments” on the break in that game

. Notable Tactical Adjustments: Throughout the season, Slot made tweaks when needed. For instance, due to Trent Alexander-Arnold’s unique skillset, Liverpool sometimes morphed into a 3-2-5 shape in possession, with Trent in midfield and Robertson forming a back three with the center-backs. At other times, especially after Trent’s spring injury, Liverpool played a more orthodox back-four with Kostas Tsimikas or Joe Gomez as traditional full-backs providing width. Slot also showed flexibility in personnel: he rotated between a strikerless front line (using Gakpo as a false nine behind wingers) versus a more classic target man approach with Núñez. These adjustments kept opponents guessing. Overall, Arne Slot’s tactical approach can be summarized as a blend of Dutch-influenced positional play with Liverpool’s hallmark intensity. The team maintained Klopp’s core principles of pressing and fast transitions but added Slot’s “mind-blowing” positional rotations and control. The result was a Liverpool side that played dynamic, attacking football while remaining defensively robust and well-conditioned for the title challenge.
Injuries and Squad Fitness

Liverpool navigated the season with a few significant injuries, but largely managed to cope due to squad depth and smart rotation. Here is a timeline of key injury issues and returns during the 2024/25 Premier League campaign:
Harvey Elliott (September 2024): Early in the season, young midfielder Harvey Elliott suffered a fractured foot while on England U21 duty

. This injury in September sidelined him for about six weeks. Elliott returned to action in November, easing back in with substitute appearances. His absence coincided with Federico Chiesa also being unavailable, slightly reducing Liverpool’s attacking midfield depth in the autumn.
Federico Chiesa (October 2024): Summer signing Federico Chiesa had limited involvement due to fitness issues. After a couple of appearances in August and September, Chiesa picked up a knock in training by early October



. He missed the Champions League game vs Bologna and the league trip to Crystal Palace, and the club was cautious with his recovery. Chiesa was “doubtful” and eventually sat out until after the October international break

. He returned to make a few cameo appearances later in the winter, but his recurring knee problems meant he never had a consistent run in the league (only 4 league appearances total).
Diogo Jota (Oct–Nov 2024): Jota had an stop-start campaign due to a couple of minor injuries. In late September, he took a knock to his foot against Wolves, which required a scan

. Though the scan was clear, he missed a few training sessions as a precaution. He was back by early October and scored against Nottingham Forest, but in November he suffered another small muscle injury that made him doubtful for a game against Brentford

. Jota’s injuries were not long-term; he usually returned within one or two weeks. However, these issues limited him to 22 league appearances. His absences saw Darwin Núñez get more minutes as striker during those periods.
Andy Robertson (Oct 2024): Robertson had a scare in late September when he came off with a knock in the Wolves match

. He missed a mid-week Champions League game as a precaution, but the issue was minor. Robertson was back in training by October 1

and did not miss any Premier League game, though Slot occasionally rested him in easier fixtures, using Kostas Tsimikas to keep Robertson fresh.
Joe Gomez (Dec 2024 – present): Versatile defender Joe Gomez sustained a serious hamstring injury in December. This issue “plagued his season since December” and ultimately required surgery

. Gomez’s last appearance was in early December, after which he was ruled out. He missed a total of 19 games by April

. The surgery and rehabilitation meant Gomez was targeting a return before season’s end, but it remained uncertain. His absence reduced depth in both center-back and right-back positions. In response, youngster Jarell Quansah saw more involvement as the fourth-choice center-back, and Conor Bradley’s role as backup right-back became important once Trent had to be rested or absent.
Trent Alexander-Arnold (March–April 2025): Trent, ever-present in the first half of the season, suffered an ankle injury in early March. He picked up the knock late in the Champions League Round of 16 second leg against PSG (on March 11). Fortunately, scans showed no severe damage



. Nevertheless, the injury kept him out for several weeks. By April 9, Trent had missed the last three league games

. Liverpool’s medical team said he was “on track with his recovery” and hoping to play again before the end of April

. There was no specific match targeted, as it depended on how his ankle responded in training. Ultimately, Alexander-Arnold returned to full training by mid-to-late April and made a substitute appearance on April 27. During his absence, Conor Bradley deputized at right-back in league matches – a challenge the 20-year-old handled relatively well, even grabbing an assist in one outing. Slot also adjusted tactically, sometimes opting for a more conservative back four with Joe Gomez (before his injury) or James Milner (if registered – Milner was not in the league squad, so likely just Bradley/Tsimikas swaps).
Alisson Becker (March–April 2025): Liverpool’s star goalkeeper Alisson suffered a concussion while on international duty with Brazil in late March

. He was withdrawn from Brazil’s squad after a collision and had to follow FA concussion protocols. Alisson missed two league games (the April fixtures vs Everton and Fulham) as he had not yet passed the full “return to play” protocol by those dates

. Caoimhín Kelleher stepped in as the starting keeper and performed solidly. Alisson resumed training normally and was “hopeful” to be cleared for the April 13 match against West Ham

. In line with the 21-day guideline for concussions

, he returned on April 13 and played that match. His absence was brief, but it highlighted Kelleher’s reliability as a backup. (Alisson also missed a couple games earlier in the season due to a minor shoulder injury in November, during which Kelleher and even third-choice keeper Vítězslav Jaroš were on standby. Notably, Jaroš made the bench and eventually a late-season appearance when Alisson was out and Kelleher had a minor knock.)
Other Injuries: Midfielder Ryan Gravenberch had a scare in March when he limped off in a Europa League match, but he was fit for the next league game – he ended up playing in all 33 league games up to April, indicating no major injuries. Luis Díaz had a brief absence in January due to illness, but no significant injury. Darwin Núñez missed one game through illness (a flu bout kept him out vs Wolves on Sep 28

) but returned immediately after. Captain Virgil van Dijk remained ever-present and injury-free in the league, a big boost to consistency at the back. Curtis Jones served a suspension in October (carrying over a red card from a cup game), which accounted for some of his missed games rather than injury.
Squad Rotation for Fitness: Arne Slot managed player fitness smartly. He often substituted Trent Alexander-Arnold and Andy Robertson in latter stages of games to keep them fresh, something Trent himself noted with a grin: “we probably play a little bit less now… he’s conserving a lot of our energy”

. By December 7, Trent had been subbed off nine times in the league

 – a clear sign of load management. Key attackers like Salah and Díaz were occasionally given rests late in games once results were secure. This rotation helped Liverpool avoid burnout and limit muscular injuries during a congested season.
In summary, Liverpool did face challenges with injuries – especially losing Joe Gomez long-term and short spells without Trent and Alisson – but they avoided the kind of injury crisis that derails a campaign. The squad’s depth meant players like Kelleher, Bradley, Quansah, and Tsimikas could fill in capably. The club’s careful approach (not rushing recoveries, using the full squad) ensured that by the run-in, only Gomez remained a significant absentee, with others back available for the final push.
Transfer Activity (Players In, Out, Loans, Academy Promotions)

Liverpool’s transfer activity around the 2024/25 season was measured, as the club opted for continuity after the Klopp era. Nevertheless, a few key changes in personnel occurred:
Managerial Change: The summer’s biggest “transfer” was managerial. Longtime boss Jürgen Klopp departed at the end of 2023/24, and Arne Slot was hired from Feyenoord (announced in May 2024)

. Slot officially took charge on June 1, 2024

. He brought in some of his own backroom staff, though core members like Pep Lijnders remained to ease the transition.
Players In: In terms of players, Liverpool had a quiet summer 2024 window with only one major signing before the season – Federico Chiesa

. The Italian winger was acquired from Juventus for a modest fee (reportedly around £20 million) due to his injury history. Chiesa was seen as an opportunistic addition to add depth and quality up front. Notably, sporting director Jörg Schmadtke (and successor Jürgen Schmadtke/Hughes) decided against multiple signings, believing the current squad was strong. Chiesa’s arrival was the sole first-team addition as the season kicked off

. In the January 2025 window, Liverpool did not make any high-profile signings either, focusing on stability with a big lead in the table. There were rumors linking a young center-back, but nothing materialized. Thus, no winter signings of note were made.
Players Out: A few veteran players left in July 2024 upon contract expiry: James Milner (ended his Liverpool stint and joined Brighton), Adrián (the reserve goalkeeper was released)

, Joël Matip, and Thiago Alcântara. Matip and Thiago both saw their Liverpool contracts run out in June 2024 and departed as free agents

. Matip, a long-serving center-half, was allowed to leave (he would later sign for a club in Saudi Arabia), and Thiago, having struggled with injuries, returned to Spain. These departures left “gaps in the squad to be filled” but Liverpool chose to fill them internally

. Also departing was young attacker Fábio Carvalho: after a loan at RB Leipzig in 2023/24, Carvalho transferred permanently to Brentford in August 2024 for a fee rising to £27.5 million

. Liverpool negotiated a sell-on clause in that deal

Selection Trends & Rotation: Arne Slot largely relied on a settled core of players, while also using rotation to keep the squad fresh across a busy schedule (which included domestic cups and Champions League). Alexis Mac Allister missed only one league game (due to a minor suspension), partnering with Gravenberch in the majority of matches. Slot rotated particularly in the attacking positions and full-back spots. Up front, he had five high-quality forwards for three spots (Salah, Díaz, Gakpo, Jota, Núñez). His selection generally depended on form and fitness:
Salah was undroppable on the right wing, given his incredible output.
Luis Díaz became the primary left winger, especially in the first half of the season, with his direct dribbling and goal threat yielding 11 league goals. When Díaz needed a rest or if Slot wanted a different look, Cody Gakpo would play on the left. Gakpo also often filled the central attacking role (either as a false-nine or a No.10) – he started many games either up top or just behind the striker.
Diogo Jota and Darwin Núñez essentially job-shared the striker position. Early in the season, Jota’s form (and Núñez’s illness) saw Jota start more games, and he scored regularly. After Jota’s injury in October, Núñez had a run of starts and performed well (notably scoring in wins vs Bournemouth and West Ham). Slot then alternated them based on opposition: Jota’s clever movement unlocked deep defenses, while Núñez was used against high lines or more physical center-halves. Both ended with similar league goal tallies (6 for Jota, 5 for Núñez), reflecting the rotation. Notably, Luis Díaz was occasionally deployed centrally in certain games – in late season, Slot even played Díaz as a false nine (a move that yielded success, as Diaz’s intelligent pressing and runs opened space; he scored a hat-trick in a UCL game and that confidence carried to league play)

However, it quickly became apparent that Gravenberch’s skill set suited the No.6 role best among the squad. By September, he was the first-choice defensive midfielder, allowing Mac Allister to play in a more natural roaming No.8 role. Dominik Szoboszlai and Curtis Jones effectively competed for the third midfield spot. Szoboszlai started the majority of matches, especially early on, bringing energy and goal threat. Jones, however, forced his way in with consistently solid displays and ended up starting roughly half the league games. In some matches (particularly big games or when chasing a goal), Slot fielded all four midfielders by pushing Szoboszlai up as a quasi-right winger or No.10, and playing Jones and Mac Allister together as dual 8s ahead of Gravenberch. This was part of the tactical fluidity – e.g., against certain opponents, Liverpool’s shape looked like a 4-4-2 diamond. Meanwhile, Harvey Elliott was unfortunately not a factor for much of the season due to his injury; upon returning, he mostly made late substitute appearances to give others rest. Wataru Endo, signed under Klopp in 2023, found opportunities scarce – he was primarily used in cup matches, with only a handful of late league cameos once games were in hand. In defense, the first-choice back four was clear: Andy Robertson (LB), Virgil van Dijk (CB), Ibrahima Konaté (CB), Trent Alexander-Arnold (RB), with Alisson in goal. Van Dijk and Konaté developed an excellent partnership, blending Van Dijk’s leadership and aerial dominance with Konaté’s speed and physicality. Gomez’s injury in December opened more chances for Quansah, who acquitted himself well when called upon. At right-back, Trent was the starter whenever fit – his creative role was crucial. Conor Bradley started several games in the second half (particularly in April when Trent was out) and held his own, demonstrating the value of his loan seasoning. At left-back, Robertson was ever-present until late in games, where Kostas Tsimikas often got 15-20 minute run-outs. Tsimikas also started a couple of matches in weeks with tight turnarounds, allowing Robbo to rest. The team’s stars delivered world-class performances, emerging talents stepped up, and the overall depth meant injuries did not derail momentum.
